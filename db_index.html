Handedness & Lifespan Risk Dashboard · Bayesian analysis of CDC 1999 mortality (2.39M US deaths) and Gilbert & Wysocki (1992) handedness rates

Business question: should an insurer's mortality model treat left-handedness as a risk factor? We test the popular belief using Bayes' rule, P(Age | Hand) = P(Hand | Age) × P(Age) / P(Hand).

Key insights

  • The headline gap falls from 5.5 years (1990) to 2.3 years (2018), so most of the "penalty" is a cohort artefact.
  • Men: 5.9 → 1.7 years. Women: 4.5 → 2.7 years. Women outlive men by about 7 years in every handedness group.
  • A residual gap remains in 2018, so the data does not prove zero effect. It shows the popular figure is greatly overstated.
